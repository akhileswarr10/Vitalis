# Onboard Guide: Orthostatic Intolerance and Cardiovascular Deconditioning

## Applies When
- Orthostatic heart rate rise exceeds 20 bpm upon transitioning from supine/rest to standing/upright posture.
- Systolic blood pressure drops by >20 mmHg or diastolic drops by >10 mmHg upon standing.
- Crew member reports lightheadedness, presyncope, tunneling vision, or weakness during re-entry preparations or mock-up vertical tasks.

## Action Steps
1. Immediately sit or lie down supine with legs elevated above heart level to facilitate venous return from the lower extremities to central circulation.
2. Engage lower body isometric counter-pressure maneuvers: cross legs and contract thigh, calf, and abdominal muscles firmly for 30-second cycles.
3. Don anti-gravity garment or graduated lower-body compression stockings (30–40 mmHg pressure gradient) before vertical translation drills.
4. Increase daily fluid intake with isotonic electrolyte rehydration drinks per NASA fluid-loading protocol (0.9% saline equivalent packets).
5. Review dietary sodium intake to promote intravascular plasma volume retention during cephalad-to-caudal fluid redistribution phases.
6. Conduct lower-body negative pressure (LBNP) acclimation sessions if device is available on the mission architecture.
7. Log resting, sitting, and standing blood pressure and pulse readings twice daily.

## Escalation Criteria
- Orthostatic heart rate increase exceeds 30 bpm sustained, or resting systolic blood pressure exceeds 160 mmHg.
- Frank syncope (loss of consciousness) or persistent ataxia preventing safe intravehicular translation.
- Signs of central circulatory compromise or cardiac arrhythmia.
- Contact flight surgeon immediately; suspend all high-workload operations and EVAs.
